# Phase 2.5 - Corneal specular highlights as an illuminant reference

A time-boxed rescue attempt for N1, generated by `scripts/phase2_5_report.py`.

**Hypothesis.** Under the dichromatic reflection model, specular reflection from a dielectric preserves the illuminant's spectral power distribution. A corneal highlight is therefore close to a direct sample of the scene illuminant and carries **no per-subject reflectance term** - precisely the variance that defeated the sclera in Phase 2, where a 10% yellowing shift moved the estimate 4.40 degrees.

**Thresholds were declared in CLAUDE.md before any of this ran.** Feasibility gate 30%; SUPPORTED requires beating grey-world at p < 0.05; PARTIALLY SUPPORTED requires beating no-correction; REFUTED otherwise.

### 1.1 Saturation is not the blocker

Clipped pixels carry no chromatic information - their channel ratios are an artefact of the sensor ceiling, not of the light - so they are excluded rigorously and the survivors counted.

The device spread is the striking number: it is larger than any device effect found in Phase 2's segmentation. Whether a usable corneal highlight exists at all depends substantially on which phone took the picture - a deployment concern independent of whether the estimate is accurate.

Lighting behaves counter-intuitively: **poor light is not the worst case**. Indoor frames detect most often but clip most (point sources); poor-light frames clip least. This is the opposite of the segmentation result in Phase 2, where poor light was clearly worst.

### 1.3 SBVPI's 0% is a segmentation artefact, not a finding

SBVPI yields **zero** iris/pupil pixels from the Phase 2 model - consistent with that phase's measured iris IoU of 0.000 on SBVPI. Its highlight rate therefore measures segmentation transfer, not highlight availability, and it is excluded from the gate denominator and reported separately rather than being averaged into a misleading headline number.

## 2. Specular illuminant estimation (Task 2)

A specular estimate was obtainable on **77.6%** of evaluated frames. Decomposition routes actually used:

| route | frames |
| --- | --- |
| pupil_highlight_direct | 862 |
| none | 403 |
| iris_highlight_direct | 273 |
| dichromatic_plane_intersection | 173 |
| degenerate_dark_surface | 85 |

The routes, in order of directness:

1. **Degenerate dark surface.** The pupil has `D ~= 0`, so `I ~= m_s * L` and its colour cloud collapses to a single direction which *is* the illuminant. A plane fit correctly refuses such data; that refusal is not a failure but the cleanest available reading, and it is used as the preferred route.
2. **Plane intersection.** Two surfaces of different body colour give two dichromatic planes, both containing `L`; their intersection is `L`. No assumption about which pixels are 'most specular' is needed.
3. **Single-surface residual.** Fallback only.

This is a genuine dichromatic decomposition, not a mean of bright pixels. Both routes are verified in `tests/test_phase2_5.py` against synthetic surfaces built from the dichromatic model with a known illuminant.

## 4. Deployment realism: a narrowing field of view (Task 4)

Grey-world needs scene context; specular and sclera references are local. A real capture may be a tight periocular crop rather than a wide scene, so grey-world is re-run on progressively tighter centre crops around the eye.

| field of view | grey-world mean dE2000 |
| --- | --- |
| 100% | 6.076 |
| 60% | 5.790 |
| 40% | 5.416 |
| 25% | 3.935 |
| 15% | 4.889 |

Crop-invariant references, for comparison:

| method | mean dE2000 |
| --- | --- |
| specular | 7.821 |
| sclera_fitted_prior | 7.427 |
| none | 9.530 |

### 4.1 The result is the opposite of the hypothesis

Grey-world was expected to degrade as scene context disappeared. It **improves**: from 6.076 dE2000 on the full frame to **3.935 at 25% field of view** - its best result anywhere in Phase 2 or 2.5, and better than every endogenous method by a wide margin.

The mechanism is straightforward once seen. A wide MOBIUS frame is dominated by facial skin, which is strongly and consistently red, so the grey-world assumption is badly violated and the estimate leans red. A tight periocular crop contains sclera, iris, pupil, lashes and a little skin - a far more balanced set of reflectances, so the grey-world assumption is *better satisfied* on exactly the narrow crop a real capture would produce.

The very tightest crop (15%) is worse again (4.889), consistent with that reading: crop far enough and the balance is lost to iris and sclera alone.

**Stated carefully: this does not merely preserve grey-world's advantage, it widens it in the deployment-realistic regime.** The Task 4 hypothesis - that a local reference would win once scene context vanished - is refuted. The honest conclusion is the inconvenient one: for this data, the cheapest possible method is also the best, and it gets better as the input gets closer to what a screening app would actually capture.

One caveat against overstatement: these crops are centred on the eye using the segmentation, so they are idealised. A real capture would be hand-framed and less well centred. The effect is large enough (+2.141 dE2000) that framing noise is unlikely to erase it, but it has not been measured here.

## 5. Verdict (Task 5)

### Specular estimation: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

Against the thresholds declared in CLAUDE.md before any of this ran:

| comparison | mean diff dE2000 | Wilcoxon p | win rate | meets threshold? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| specular vs no correction | -1.709 | 2.33e-04 | 29/40 | **yes** |
| specular vs grey-world | +1.745 | 4.53e-06 | 5/40 | **no** |

Specular estimation removes real capture-condition variation - it beats no correction convincingly. It does **not** beat grey-world, so under the pre-declared criterion it is PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, not supported.

### The rescue did not rescue anything

The point of Phase 2.5 was that a specular reference carries no per-subject reflectance term, and should therefore beat the sclera. It does not:

| comparison | mean diff | Wilcoxon p | result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| specular vs sclera-referenced (Phase 2) | +0.394 dE2000 | 0.29 | **no significant difference** |

**Specular estimation is statistically indistinguishable from the method it was designed to replace.** Removing the per-subject reflectance term - the quantity Phase 2 identified as the bottleneck - did not improve the result. That is informative: it means inter-individual reflectance variance was not the *only* thing limiting the sclera, or that the specular route introduces compensating errors of its own (detection is available on only 78% of frames, 41% of frames carry multiple light sources, and highlight availability varies 39 percentage points across devices).

### N1 as a whole: **REFUTED**

Two independent endogenous ocular references have now been tested against the same protocol, and neither beats a whole-image statistic that requires no segmentation, no reference surface, and no anatomy:

| method | mean dE2000 on held-out iris | vs grey-world |
| --- | --- | --- |
| grey_world | 6.076 | - |
| specular_plus_sclera | 7.047 | +0.971 |
| sclera_fitted_prior | 7.427 | +1.351 |
| specular | 7.821 | +1.745 |
| none | 9.530 | +3.455 |

And the deployment-realism check, which was expected to favour local references, went decisively the other way - see section 4.

**The contribution of N1 is therefore the diagnosis, not the method.** Stated plainly and without hedging: *endogenous ocular white references - both the sclera and the corneal specular highlight - fail to support calibration-free colour correction on mobile captures, and they fail for a measured reason. The sclera fails because its diffuse reflectance varies between individuals by more than the illuminant signal being estimated (a 10% yellowing shift moves the estimate 4.40 degrees, larger than grey-world's entire error), while segmentation accuracy and estimation mathematics are both exonerated (mask perturbation of 31 pixels costs 0.40 degrees; estimation given a known reference reflectance achieves 0.67 degrees across six sensors). The corneal highlight removes that per-subject term and still does not improve on the sclera, while adding its own failure modes: availability varying 39 percentage points across devices and multiple light sources in 41% of frames.*

That is a quantified, mechanistic account of why a plausible and repeatedly proposed idea does not work. It is a legitimate contribution and is written up as one.

### What survives

* **N1a stands**: given a reference surface of known reflectance, illuminant estimation reaches 0.666 degrees across six sensors and transfers leave-one-camera-out. The mathematics is sound; the problem is the reference.
* **The segmentation pipeline stands**: sclera IoU 0.875, and it runs on Eyes-Defy with a 0% failure rate. Phases 4-8 can use it.
* **Grey-world on a tight periocular crop is the practical recommendation** for any colour normalisation this project needs downstream - it is the best measured method and the cheapest.
