## 1. Why it fails: the signal is smaller than the calibration noise

The clearest statement of the result does not need the inversion at all. Ask how much tissue colour actually changes per unit of haemoglobin, and compare it to the colour error the calibration leaves:

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| **signal** - simulated, mean over Hb 4-18 | **0.452 dE2000 per g/dL** |
| **signal** - empirical, real Eyes-Defy conjunctiva | **~0.70 dE2000 per g/dL** |
| **noise** - grey-world @25% FOV (best measured) | **3.935 dE2000** |
| **noise** - grey-world full frame | 6.076 dE2000 |
| noise / signal (empirical) | **~5.6x** |
| noise / signal (simulated) | 8.7x |

**The calibration residual alone is equivalent to roughly 5.6-8.7 g/dL of haemoglobin error. The entire clinically meaningful range is 14 g/dL wide.**

### The model was checked against real tissue

The signal figure is the load-bearing number, so it was not left to the model. Conjunctival colour was measured directly on **216 Eyes-Defy subjects** using the dataset's own palpebral masks, binned by haemoglobin, and the bin-to-bin CIEDE2000 change computed. That gives **~0.70 dE2000 per g/dL** against the model's 0.452 - the same order, with the model slightly conservative.

Two caveats, both of which make the empirical figure an *upper* bound on the true haemoglobin signal:

* bin-to-bin colour differences in real data also contain inter-subject, capture-condition and device variation, not only haemoglobin;
* the lowest bin (n=3 subjects) produced 5.26 dE2000/g/dL, which is sampling noise, and is excluded from the quoted figure.

Using the larger empirical value rather than the model's own is the conservative choice: it makes the gate *easier* to pass, and it still fails.

## 2. The error is NOT worse at low haemoglobin - it is better

Mean absolute error at Hb <= 8 g/dL: **1.900 g/dL**. At Hb >= 14: **4.645 g/dL**. Ratio **0.41x**.

This is the one genuinely favourable finding in the phase, and it has a physical cause: the reflectance-versus-haemoglobin curve saturates, so colour changes about five times faster per g/dL at Hb 4 (1.013 dE2000/g/dL) than at Hb 16 (0.203). **The measurement is most sensitive exactly where screening decisions are made.**

It is not enough. Even at its most sensitive the method needs calibration roughly four times better than the best measured (3.935 vs the ~1.0 that Hb 4 would require), and the mean error below 8 g/dL is still 1.90 g/dL - inside the MARGINAL band at best.

## 3. Task 4: how much prior error is tolerable (the question Phase 2 could not answer)

Phase 2 established that MOBIUS self-consistency is mathematically blind to the reflectance prior - a prior is constant per subject, so it shifts a subject's colour cluster without changing its spread. Simulation is not blind to it. With the illuminant held **perfect**, each tissue prior is perturbed alone and the resulting haemoglobin error measured.

| prior | nominal | stays within +/-1.0 g/dL for | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| blood volume fraction | 0.06 | 0.045 - 0.10 (about +/-30%) | tolerable |
| oxygenation StO2 | 0.75 | 0.60 - 1.00 (the entire range) | **benign** |
| layer thickness scale | 1.0 | 0.8 - 2.0 | tolerable |
| **melanin fraction** | 0.0 | **0.0 only** | **catastrophic** |

### Melanin is a catastrophic confounder, and this is an N5 finding

| melanin volume fraction | resulting Hb error |
| --- | --- |
| 0.000 | -0.00 g/dL |
| 0.005 | +9.60 g/dL |
| 0.010 | +12.00 g/dL |
| 0.020 | +12.00 g/dL |
| 0.030 | +12.00 g/dL |
| 0.050 | +12.00 g/dL |

**A melanin volume fraction of 0.005 - half of one percent - shifts the recovered haemoglobin by +9.6 g/dL.** At 0.01 and above the estimate rails to the top of the search range, i.e. complete failure rather than a calibrated error.

Conjunctival melanin is low but not zero, and conjunctival melanosis is more prevalent in darker-skinned populations. **This is a direct, quantified mechanism by which a colour-based haemoglobin estimator would be severely biased by skin tone** - which is precisely what N5 exists to audit. It is recorded here because it was found here, and it should shape N5 rather than being rediscovered later.

Two honest qualifications: the epithelial melanin layer in this model is thin, so the sensitivity may be overstated by the layered approximation; and the estimate railing to the range edge means the magnitude above 0.01 is not meaningful, only the fact of failure. Neither changes the direction.

By contrast **oxygenation is benign across its whole physiological range**, which is a useful negative: StO2 does not need to be known or controlled.

## 4. Optical constants and their verification status

Every constant is cited in `src/hemosight/simulation/constants.py` - haemoglobin extinction from the Prahl compilation of Gratzer and Kollias, scattering from Jacques 2013, melanin from Jacques 1998, water from Hale and Querry 1973, layer thicknesses from Efron 2009 and Zhivov 2006.

> ⚠️ **VERIFICATION REQUIRED BEFORE PUBLICATION.** The spectral tables are TRANSCRIBED from those published compilations. This environment has no network access and no installed package ships haemoglobin optical data, so they have not been read from the primary files. Every downstream number is provisional until they are re-verified.

They are internally consistency-checked, which is not the same as verified but does catch gross error:

| check | result |
| --- | --- |
| all 6 haemoglobin isosbestic points within 12 nm | **pass** |
| HbO2 visible absorption peak | 576 nm (pass, expect 542/577) |
| deoxy/oxy absorption ratio 650-700 nm | 8.36x (pass) |

**This check earned its place.** On first run it failed: the 500 nm isosbestic landed at 515 nm, and the 529/545 pair had collapsed because HbO2's 542 nm alpha peak was not rising above deoxy-Hb between them. Both were transcription errors in the table, and both were corrected. All six points now land within 0.5 nm.

### What this model does NOT capture

* **Specular surface reflection and the tear film** - a wet ocular surface has a strong specular component that no diffuse model represents.
* **Vessel geometry** - blood is treated as a homogeneous volume fraction, not as discrete vessels with their own diameter-dependent absorption.
* **Inter-individual anatomy** - one layer stack with scaled thicknesses, not the real distribution of conjunctival structure across people.
* **Inter-layer radiative transfer** - layers are combined by double-pass attenuation rather than full transport, and the Monte Carlo that would have measured that approximation error was not built (Task 1, stopped).
* **The six nus8 cameras' spectral sensitivities** - these are NOT available. NUS ships illuminants and colorchecker coordinates, not SSFs, and no installed package carries them. The CIE 1931 observer was used as a proxy.

## 5. Verdict on N2

### N2: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED - and its purpose has changed**

N2 claims a Monte Carlo forward model that supplies reliably labelled severe anemia. The minimal forward model built for the gate is validated where it can be (isosbestic structure, monotonic and physically-correct response to haemoglobin, agreement with real conjunctival colour to within a factor of ~1.5) and it does what a forward model is for.

But **the full Monte Carlo simulator of Tasks 1-3 was not built, and on this evidence should not be**. Its stated purpose was to generate training data for an RGB-to-haemoglobin inversion. The gate shows that inversion cannot survive the calibration error available, so better training data does not help: the limit is not data, it is that the signal is smaller than the noise floor.

**What the forward model has already earned is its role as an analysis instrument rather than a data generator.** In one phase it produced the gate result, the signal-to-noise framing, the low-Hb sensitivity finding, and the melanin confounder - none of which required a photon-transport simulation.

## 6. What this means for the project

Stated plainly, because it is the most consequential finding so far:

**The primary claim (N3+N4 - physically-grounded haemoglobin estimation in g/dL with a calibrated interval) is not achievable from RGB photographs at the calibration accuracy this project can reach.** Haemoglobin changes tissue colour by roughly 0.5-0.7 dE2000 per g/dL; the best available calibration leaves 3.9. The measurement is buried in the calibration residual.

Three directions remain open, and the choice between them is the user's:

1. **Abandon the point estimate, keep N4.** A three-state screen (negative / refer / cannot decide) with honest conformal intervals may still be useful even when those intervals are several g/dL wide - an abstention-heavy screener that refuses most cases is a legitimate, publishable negative-leaning result, and N4's machinery is exactly what quantifies it.
2. **Change the input.** The PPG dataset measures haemoglobin through a fundamentally better-conditioned channel - four narrow wavelengths with a controlled source, no ambient illuminant to estimate. The gate's failure is specific to *ambient-light RGB photography*, not to non-invasive haemoglobin estimation in general.
3. **Report the whole thing as a negative result.** The project would then consist of three rigorous, quantified refutations - endogenous white references (Phase 2/2.5), and the colour-to-haemoglobin inversion itself (Phase 3) - each with a mechanism. That is a more honest contribution than most of the literature this project set out to improve on, much of which reports single-site accuracy without cross-device validation.

What should **not** happen is proceeding to Phases 4-8 as though the gate passed.
