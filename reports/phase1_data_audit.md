# Phase 1 - Data Audit
Generated by `scripts/phase1_audit.py` from `data/interim/manifests/` and `data/interim/overlap/`. `data/raw/` was read only.
> **Headline:** three findings below change what this project can claim. CP-AnemiC is largely *contained inside* the Ghana conjunctiva set; CP-AnemiC's haemoglobin labels are internally inconsistent on its duplicated images; and the two largest conjunctiva sets are pre-segmented crops with **no sclera**, which is the input N1 requires.

### 2.3 Haemoglobin distribution and severe anemia
Severe anemia **does exist** in this collection, contrary to the assumption recorded in novelty claim N2.

| dataset | n_with_hb | hb_min | hb_max | n_lt_7 (severe) | n_lt_5 (very severe) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cp_anemic | 710 | 3.10 | 15.00 | 48.00 | 12.00 |
| eyes_defy | 217 | 7.00 | 17.40 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| hb_ppg | 252 | 8.50 | 17.30 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

CP-AnemiC severity labels: Non-Anemic=286, Moderate=232, Mild=144, Severe=48.

**Consequence for N2.** The claim that "no public dataset contains severe anemia" is too strong as written and must be narrowed. CP-AnemiC contains 48 images below 7 g/dL (minimum 3.1 g/dL). The defensible version of N2 is that severe cases are *rare and concentrated in a single site* - and, per section 3, those very images carry unreliable labels.

### 2.4 Image content: are these photographs, or crops?
Fraction of near-black pixels (max channel < 12), sampled 60 images per dataset, seed 0. A high value means the image is a segmented cutout on a black field rather than a photograph of a scene.

| dataset | n_sampled | black_mean | black_median | pct_images_over_30pct_black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cp_anemic | 60 | 0.705 | 0.719 | 100.000 |
| ghana_conj | 60 | 0.723 | 0.722 | 100.000 |
| ghana_nail | 60 | 0.205 | 0.189 | 18.333 |
| eyes_defy | 60 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| sbvpi | 60 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| mobius | 60 | 0.019 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

**This is the most consequential finding for N1.** CP-AnemiC and the Ghana conjunctiva
set are *pre-segmented conjunctiva cutouts*: every sampled image is more than half
black, and the eye surrounding the conjunctiva - including the sclera - has been
removed. N1 estimates the scene illuminant from the sclera as an endogenous white
reference. **That input does not exist in these two datasets.**

What N1 can still be built and validated on:

* `nus8` - illuminant estimation accuracy against ground truth, 6 cameras. Intact.
* `SBVPI` / `MOBIUS` - sclera segmentation, full periocular images. Intact. No Hb.
* `eyes_defy` - the only dataset with full photographs **and** haemoglobin, so the
  only end-to-end test of "sclera white reference -> Hb". **218 subjects, 2 sites,
  2 near-identical phone models, no severe cases (minimum 7.0 g/dL).**

So the headline claim rests on 218 subjects, not on the ~9,000 images the collection
appears to offer. This should be stated in the paper rather than discovered by a
reviewer.

### 3.1 CP-AnemiC is contained inside the Ghana conjunctiva set

| Signal | Result |
| --- | --- |
| MD5 distinct hashes shared | **419** of 498 CP-AnemiC unique images (**84.1%**) |
| CP-AnemiC files byte-identical to a Ghana file | **620 / 710** (87.3%) |
| pHash Hamming = 0 | 646 / 710 |
| pHash Hamming <= 10 | **697 / 710 (98.17%)** |
| dHash Hamming <= 10 | 708 / 710 (99.72%) |
| Embedding cosine >= 0.92 | 701 / 710 (98.73%) |

All four independent signals agree. See
`reports/figures/phase1_overlap_cp_anemic__ghana_conj.png` for side-by-side pairs at
Hamming distance 0.

**Verdict: these are not two sites. CP-AnemiC is a labelled subset of the same
collection.** Only 14.2% of the Ghana conjunctiva files are covered, so the
Ghana set is the superset.

### 3.2 Cross body site: an N6 opportunity, not contamination

Image content does **not** overlap between conjunctiva and fingernail images
(MD5 shared = 0, minimum pHash distance = 10,
maximum embedding cosine = 0.939) - as expected for photographs of
different body parts. Image hashing cannot answer the participant question here.

The participant evidence is the numbering. Subject numbers parsed from filenames:

| Class | Conjunctiva subjects | Nail subjects | Shared numbers | Jaccard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| non-anemic | 204 (1-204) | 204 (1-204) | **204** | **1.000** |
| anemic | 251 (1-272) | 271 (1-272) | 250 | 0.919 |

A perfect 204/204 correspondence over a contiguous range is not plausible by chance.
**Roughly 454 participants appear at both body sites.** Flagged as an **N6
opportunity**: genuine per-subject conjunctiva+nail pairs, which is what multimodal
fusion needs and what an ensemble over unrelated subjects cannot provide.

Caveat: this is inferred from a shared numbering scheme, not from a participant ID
column. It is strong but not proof, and these subjects have **no Hb values** - only a
binary class - so the pairs support fusion architecture work, not calibrated
regression.

### 3.3 CP-AnemiC haemoglobin labels are internally inconsistent

CP-AnemiC has 710 files but only **498 unique images**.
Of the 91 groups of byte-identical images, **90 carry more than one
distinct haemoglobin value**. The worst group is one image appearing 10 times with
haemoglobin recorded as 4.6, 5.1, 8.0, 8.7, 8.8, 8.9, 9.5, 9.7, 10.5 and 10.93 g/dL,
attributed to 8 different hospitals.

303 of 710 files (42.7%) sit in a conflicted group.

Either the same photograph was reused across participant records, or the
image-to-record linkage is broken. Either way **the per-image Hb target cannot be
trusted on the duplicated subset**, and this is the subset containing most of the
severe cases. This is a labelling-integrity problem, independent of the overlap
finding, and it constrains every Hb regression result this project can report.

### 3.4 Signal that could not be computed
Metadata correspondence on (Hb, age, sex) could NOT be computed. CP-AnemiC is the only one of the three that ships per-subject metadata; the Ghana conjunctiva and fingernail sets provide no Hb, age or sex, only an anemic/non-anemic class in the filename. This signal is unavailable, not negative.

## 5. SCIN: removed from the project
`data/raw/scin-main/` held **no data** - 5 documentation files (README, schema, licence, contributing guide, demo notebook). SCIN images and its skin-tone labels live in a Google Cloud Storage bucket, never downloaded.

Even if downloaded it could not have served N5: SCIN is **dermatology** imagery with no conjunctiva photographs, no haemoglobin, and no participant correspondence to any anemia dataset here, so its Fitzpatrick and Monk labels cannot be attached to these subjects. Using it would have required an unvalidated cross-domain transfer.

**Phase 1.5 deleted it and rebuilt N5 without it** - skin tone is now estimated by Individual Typology Angle on N1-calibrated periocular skin. See `reports/phase1_5_remediation.md`.
