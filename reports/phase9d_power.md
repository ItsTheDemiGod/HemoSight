# Phase 9D - power analysis: what this study could have detected

Generated by `scripts/power_analysis_report_phase9d.py` from `data/interim/phase9d/power.json`. The method, the one deciding comparison per arm, the clinical yardsticks and the verdict rule were **pre-declared in CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-20 before any number here was computed**.

> ## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH
>
> **Both regression comparisons are adequately powered; the imaging screening comparisons are not, and the project's most load-bearing finding is its least powered.** For haemoglobin error in g/dL this study could detect improvements of **0.18 g/dL** (imaging) and **0.14 g/dL** (PPG) at 80% power - far smaller than the **1.0 g/dL** that the narrowest WHO band would require, so *no effect large enough to matter clinically could have been missed*. For the screening comparisons the smallest detectable specificity gain is **0.116** within site, against a pre-declared clinically meaningful margin of **0.10** - marginally underpowered - and **0.314** in the italy->india cross-site direction, **three times the margin**. The cross-site collapse is the result that governs the imaging verdict, and it rests on the least powered comparison in the project. The PPG arm splits: its MAE comparison is the best-powered in the project, its specificity comparison is adequately powered, and its AUROC comparison is not (MDE 80% = 0.27 on 18 anaemic subjects).

## 1. What was computed, and what was deliberately not

**Computed: the minimum detectable effect (MDE).** MDE = (z_0.975 + z_power) * SE(paired difference), two-sided, alpha = 0.05. The multiplier is 2.802 at 80% power and 3.242 at 90%. The MDE answers *what effect size could this design have detected, given its sample size and the variance structure of its own measurements*.

**NOT computed: observed, retrospective or post-hoc power** - power recomputed at the effect size that was actually observed. This is a known statistical error, and it is worth saying why rather than just naming it. Observed power is a deterministic function of the observed p-value, so it contains no information the p-value does not already carry; and because it is low by construction whenever a result is null, reporting it would hand every negative finding in this project a built-in excuse. `hemosight.evaluation.power` contains no such function, by design. **A reader can therefore tell exactly which analysis was run: the design's detectable effect, not the study's luck.**

**Two caveats that travel with the MDE and are not corrected for.** SE is estimated from this sample and carries its own sampling uncertainty; and for sensitivity and specificity the SE depends mildly on the true effect size; no correction is applied. The MDE does use this sample to estimate a nuisance *variance* - that is standard, and is not the same error as using the observed *effect*.

**A clarification that prevents a misreading.** An effect can be statistically significant and still smaller than the MDE at 80% power: significance needs about 1.96 x SE, while 80% power needs 2.80 x SE. So Phase 9A's image-CNN specificity gain (+0.096, CI excluding zero) is real on its own terms and simultaneously below this arm's MDE(80%) of 0.116. The two statements are about different questions and neither corrects the other.

**Pairing.** Every comparison scores two models on the same subjects, so every difference here is paired. For the MAE comparisons the exact paired SE sd(d)/sqrt(n) is used; for sensitivity and specificity at a nested operating point no closed form applies, so the SE is the SD of the 2000-replicate subject-level paired bootstrap, the identical scheme (seed 20260911) Phase 9A used to produce the intervals those verdicts rest on. Pairing matters materially: on the imaging MAE comparison the per-subject errors of the two models correlate at r = 0.62, which makes the paired SE 38% smaller than an unpaired one and the MDE correspondingly tighter. An unpaired SE would have overstated every MDE in this report.

**The comparisons are the ones that decided the arms.** Model scores, folds and baselines are rebuilt by importing Phase 9A's own construction functions, and the script asserts it reproduces Phase 9A's recorded point differences before reporting anything: image-CNN MAE 1.3012 and baseline 1.2735; PPG deep 1.1126 and sex alone 0.8309; every screening difference to within 5e-3.

## 3. Per arm, in clinical units

### 3.1 Imaging regression - ADEQUATELY POWERED

n = 216 Hb-labelled Eyes-Defy subjects. The deciding comparison is the one Phase 6.5 and Phase 9A actually used: image-CNN MAE against the site-inclusive demographic baseline. Paired SE 0.0645 g/dL (bootstrap SE for reference 0.0637), giving **MDE(80%) = 0.181 g/dL** and MDE(90%) = 0.209 g/dL.

**In clinical units.** 0.18 g/dL is roughly a fifth of the narrowest WHO severity band (the 1.0 g/dL mild band for men, 10.0-10.9). An MAE improvement that small **cannot move any WHO boundary**, and it cannot move a gate band either (VIABLE < 1.0, MARGINAL 1.0-2.0). So the design was sensitive to improvements an order of magnitude smaller than the smallest one that could change a clinical decision. **The negative result is informative evidence of absence:** had the photograph carried a clinically useful amount of haemoglobin information beyond site, sex and age, this sample would have found it.

### 3.2 Imaging screening, within site - marginally UNDERPOWERED

n = 216, 91 anaemic (125 not), the Phase 9A cohort. Both halves of the pre-declared margin rule are reported.

| comparison | SE | MDE @80% | MDE @90% | vs the 0.10 margin | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| specificity gain at matched sensitivity (image-CNN) | 0.0415 | 0.116 | 0.135 | 1.16x the margin | UNDERPOWERED |
| specificity gain at matched sensitivity (mean-CIELAB) | 0.0404 | 0.113 | 0.131 | 1.13x the margin | UNDERPOWERED |
| sensitivity gain at matched specificity (image-CNN) | 0.0407 | 0.114 | 0.132 | 1.14x the margin | UNDERPOWERED |

**In clinical units.** At 80% power the smallest detectable specificity gain, 0.116, corresponds to sparing 15 of 125 non-anaemic subjects a needless blood test; the smallest detectable sensitivity gain, 0.114, to detecting 10 more of 91 anaemic subjects - about 1 additional case per 21 people screened.

**Verdict, applied as declared: UNDERPOWERED - but by a small margin, and the statement must be precise.** MDE(80%) is 0.116 against a yardstick of 0.100, i.e. 16% over. What the design could **not** reliably detect is an effect sitting exactly at the 0.10 margin: an effect of precisely that size would have been found 67% of the time, not 80%. What the design **could** detect it did: the mean-CIELAB model's observed specificity gain of +0.192 is well above its own MDE of 0.113, and Phase 9A recorded it with a CI excluding zero. **So this is not a case of a null result resting on thin power** - the arm found a real within-site effect. The honest limitation is narrower: a *borderline* effect, one just large enough to matter under the project's own margin, could have been missed.

### 3.3 Imaging cross-site - UNDERPOWERED, and this is the load-bearing finding

**Reported per direction and never pooled.** Each direction trains on one site and tests on the other, so each uses a fraction of the cohort - and the two fractions are very differently composed, which matters more than the raw n.

| direction | n | anaemic | non-anaemic | spec-gain SE | MDE @80% | MDE @90% | vs 0.10 | AUROC MDE @80% | MAE MDE @80% | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| italy to india | 95 | 68 | 27 | 0.1119 | **0.314** | 0.363 | **3.1x** | 0.181 | 0.383 g/dL | **UNDERPOWERED** |
| india to italy | 121 | 23 | 98 | 0.0609 | **0.171** | 0.197 | **1.7x** | 0.185 | 0.368 g/dL | **UNDERPOWERED** |

**The italy->india direction is the least powered comparison in the project.** MDE(80%) = 0.314 specificity, **3.1 times** the pre-declared clinically meaningful margin. The cause is visible in the table and is not simply n: of 95 test subjects 68 are anaemic and only 27 are not, so **specificity is estimated on 27 subjects**. A specificity comparison on 27 people cannot be precise, whatever the total sample says.

**In clinical units**, the smallest detectable specificity gain in that direction corresponds to sparing only 8 of 27 non-anaemic subjects a needless test - i.e. the design could only have detected an effect far larger than the one the project declared worth having.

**What this does and does not do to the recorded verdict.** Phase 9A's cross-site result was **not** a null finding: in italy->india the model **flagged 27 of 68 anaemic subjects and missed 41** - a count, carrying no estimation uncertainty at all - which is a large, directly observed failure, not an absence of evidence. The rate behind that count is sensitivity 0.397, 95% CI [0.278, 0.514]; in the other direction 57 of 98 non-anaemic subjects were needlessly flagged, specificity 0.418, CI [0.323, 0.516] (Phase 9E computed the specificity intervals Phase 9A did not store). **Prefer the counts, and attach the interval wherever a rate is used.** A power analysis constrains what *null* comparisons can be concluded from; it does not weaken an observed collapse. **But the honest statement is uncomfortable and is made anyway:** the comparison carrying the most weight in this project - the one that turned a within-site success into a non-deployable verdict - is also its least precisely estimated, and the *size* of the cross-site penalty is correspondingly uncertain even though its *direction* is not. Anyone continuing this line of work should treat the cross-site magnitude as poorly pinned down and collect more sites before quoting it. **Phase 9E says how many, and reports two figures rather than one:** specificity in that direction rests on 27 non-anaemic subjects and needs **266** to reach 80% power at the 0.10 margin on the 1/sqrt(n) reference, or **778** on the slope measured by subsampling - roughly a 936- or 2,736-person test site at that direction's prevalence, 532 or 1,555 recruited to a balanced one. The reference is the headline because that subsample fit is weak (R2 0.25), by a floor added mid-run and logged as a deviation; **size against the larger unless the scaling is measured on a pilot**. See `reports/phase9e_boundaries.md`.

### 3.4 PPG - ADEQUATELY POWERED on MAE, UNDERPOWERED on screening AUROC

n = 252 subjects with venous HemoCue reference, 18 anaemic - prevalence **7.1%**, which is what drives the split verdict.

| comparison | SE | MDE @80% | MDE @90% | yardstick | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| deep-model MAE vs sex alone | 0.0491 | **0.137 g/dL** | 0.159 g/dL | 1.0 g/dL | **ADEQUATELY POWERED** |
| specificity gain at matched sensitivity vs demographics | 0.0260 | **0.073** | 0.084 | 0.10 | **ADEQUATELY POWERED** |
| AUROC vs demographics | 0.0964 | 0.270 | 0.313 | *no pre-declared yardstick* | *not used for the arm verdict* |

**The MAE comparison is the best-powered in the project**: MDE(80%) = 0.137 g/dL on 252 subjects with the project's best reference standard, against a 1.0 g/dL yardstick. Phase 4.5's negative result - the raw-waveform model losing to sex alone - is therefore **informative**: an improvement of even 0.14 g/dL would have been detected, and none of that size exists.

**The screening AUROC comparison is badly powered, and the reason is prevalence, not n.** With only 18 anaemic subjects of 252, the paired AUROC SE is 0.0964 and MDE(80%) = 0.270 - an AUROC gap of nearly 0.27 would be needed for reliable detection. Phase 9A's recorded PPG AUROC difference of -0.0002 with CI [-0.195, +0.189] is therefore **consistent with anything from a moderate real effect to none at all**, and the arm's AUROC-based conclusion should be read as *uninformative on its own*. It is the MAE comparison and the number-needed-to-screen arithmetic (NNS 15.8 vs 14.0 for referring everybody) that carry the PPG verdict, not the AUROC. The specificity comparison, by contrast, is adequately powered (MDE(80%) = 0.073) because it is estimated on the 234 non-anaemic subjects, of which there are many.

## 4. Verdicts, and what they change

| arm | verdict | the sentence the write-up must carry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Imaging regression | **ADEQUATELY POWERED** | MDE(80%) 0.18 g/dL against a 1.0 g/dL clinical yardstick - the negative result is informative evidence of absence |
| Imaging screening, within site | **UNDERPOWERED** (marginally) | MDE(80%) 0.116 against 0.10 - a borderline effect could have been missed, though the effect the arm did find was comfortably detectable |
| Imaging cross-site | **UNDERPOWERED** | MDE(80%) 0.314 (3.1x the margin) in italy->india on 27 non-anaemic subjects - the magnitude of the cross-site penalty is poorly pinned down, though its direction was directly observed |
| PPG regression | **ADEQUATELY POWERED** | MDE(80%) 0.14 g/dL against 1.0 g/dL - the best-powered comparison in the project; the negative result is informative |
| PPG screening (AUROC) | **UNDERPOWERED** | MDE(80%) 0.27 AUROC on 18 anaemic subjects - the AUROC comparison is uninformative alone |

**What this phase changes: nothing about any recorded verdict.** It was not run to reinterpret a result, and it does not. Every MAE and screening number stands exactly as recorded. What it adds is the second half of each negative sentence - *and we could have detected an effect of size Y* - which those sentences previously lacked.

**Where it connects to the project's own framing.** Phase 7's `statistical_vs_clinical.md` established that this project's one real effect is statistically significant and clinically useless. The power analysis closes the symmetric gap: for the two regression arms the effects that were *too small to detect* are also **far too small to matter**, which is why their negative results are informative rather than merely inconclusive. An effect below 0.18 g/dL of MAE is not a missed opportunity; it is a quantity no clinical decision could use.

**What the phase does not establish.** MDEs are computed at the variance structure of these samples, and a different cohort with different prevalence or different between-subject spread would have different MDEs. The cross-site MDEs in particular are estimated on small subsets and are themselves imprecise. No MDE here is a statement about any future study's power.
