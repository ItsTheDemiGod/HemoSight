### The algebra is right and the implementation is right

A negative result on a method that is *algebraically* guaranteed deserves proof that the machinery works before the conclusion is accepted. Applying a synthetic diagonal illuminant change to a real frame and re-measuring the ratio:

| region summariser | ratio spread across synthetic illuminants |
| --- | --- |
| plain mean (exactly diagonal-invariant) | **0.000000 dE2000** |
| robust percentile-trimmed mean (as used) | 0.046 dE2000 |

**The illuminant cancels exactly, to machine precision.** The observed spread on real captures is 6.399 dE2000 - **139x larger than the implementation's own residual**. So the illuminant genuinely does cancel, and almost none of the real variation between captures is an illuminant change.

(The 0.046 residual on the trimmed summariser is itself explainable: rescaling channels changes which pixels fall inside the luminance percentile window, so a slightly different pixel set is averaged. Negligible here, but worth knowing.)

## 2. Why it fails - two mechanisms, both measured

### 2.1 The reference is noisier within a subject than between subjects

The ratio method does not need the sclera to be the same across people. It needs it to be the same across captures **of one person**. Those are different requirements, so they were measured separately:

| dataset | within-subject dE2000 | between-subject dE2000 | within/between | regime |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MOBIUS (3 phones x 3 lighting) | 5.860 | 4.050 | **1.447** | **unusable** |
| SBVPI (studio) | 1.685 | 1.825 | **0.924** | marginal |

**On phone captures the sclera varies MORE across captures of the same person (5.860) than it does between different people (4.050).** The ratio 1.447 is the wrong side of 1. A reference that is noisier than the population it normalises against is not a reference - dividing by it adds more variance than it removes, which is exactly what the Task 1 table shows.

Under controlled studio conditions (SBVPI) within-subject variation falls **3.5x**, from 5.860 to 1.685, and the ratio improves to 0.924. That is informative: the instability is dominated by capture conditions - geometry, gaze, specular contamination, focus - not by the tissue changing. But even in a studio the ratio is only 0.92, still not the << 1 regime the method needs.

### 2.2 von Kries diagonality is breaking down

Under a true diagonal model a within-image ratio cancels the illuminant **and** the camera's per-channel gains, so the phone effect should largely disappear. It does not:

| method | phone variance fraction | lighting | between-subject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| uncorrected_sclera | **0.202** | 0.232 | 0.288 |
| greyworld_sclera | **0.177** | 0.356 | 0.255 |
| iris_over_sclera | **0.177** | 0.204 | 0.390 |
| pupil_over_sclera | **0.213** | 0.220 | 0.181 |
| pupil_over_iris | **0.359** | 0.174 | 0.198 |

The ratio leaves the phone effect at **0.177**, against 0.202 uncorrected and 0.177 for grey-world - essentially unchanged. Real camera sensitivities overlap enough that an illuminant change is not a per-channel gain, so the sensor-specific error survives a transformation that should have removed it. This is the signature the phase was designed to look for.

### 2.3 The ratio amplifies signal, but amplifies noise more

| quantity | absolute colour | ratio feature | change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| signal, dE2000 per g/dL | 0.452 | 0.637 | **1.41x better** |
| noise, within-subject dE2000 | 3.330 (grey-world) | 6.399 | **1.92x worse** |
| net equivalent residual, g/dL | 5.23 | 10.04 | **1.92x worse** |

The ratio does raise the haemoglobin signal, by 1.4x. It raises the noise by 1.9x. Reporting only the first would have been the easy mistake here; the net effect is what matters and it is negative.

## 3. Would a per-subject offset help? No, twice over

**First: it removes the wrong term.** An offset subtracts a per-subject mean, which eliminates the *between*-subject variation and leaves the *within*-subject variation untouched. On MOBIUS that means removing 4.050 dE2000 and leaving **5.860** - it removes the smaller term and leaves the larger one. The problem is not that people differ; it is that one person's own captures differ.

**Second: it is not obtainable.** A per-subject offset requires at least one capture of that subject with a KNOWN answer - a reference haemoglobin measurement, or a calibrated colour target in frame. In a screening deployment neither exists: the whole premise is a first, uncalibrated capture of a person whose haemoglobin is unknown. The offset is therefore NOT obtainable, and any performance figure that assumes it is reporting an oracle, not a method.

### Melanin: confirmed to be a fitted power law, not measured data

Fitting `mu_a = A * lambda^-k` to the spectralLIB melanin curve gives **A = 6.6e+11, k = 3.330**, with a maximum log-residual of **0.000000**. It is *exactly* a power law - there is no measured structure in it at all.

Combined with the Phase 3 finding that a melanin volume fraction of 0.005 shifts recovered haemoglobin by **+9.6 g/dL**, this is now the single most consequential assumption in the model: a quantity the result is extremely sensitive to, which varies substantially between individuals, and which is known only through a smooth fit. It is flagged in `constants.py` as something to **sweep, never fix**, and it is the mechanism by which this method would be biased by skin tone.

## 5. Verdict

### The ratio reformulation: **REFUTED**

### The imaging arm of this project: **definitively a negative result**

Both formulations of the imaging claim have now been tested and both have failed, each with a measured mechanism:

| formulation | phase | result | mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Absolute illuminant recovery from the sclera | 2 | REFUTED | loses to grey-world; p ~ 3e-8 |
| Absolute illuminant recovery from corneal specular | 2.5 | REFUTED | no better than sclera, p = 0.29 |
| Absolute colorimetric Hb inversion | 3 | NOT RECOVERABLE | signal 0.45 (sim) / 0.84 (measured) dE2000/g/dL vs 3.9 noise |
| Illuminant-free within-image ratio | 3.5 | **REFUTED** | reference noisier within-subject than between; diagonality fails |

The negative result is now comprehensive rather than partial. The project has tested the two available reference surfaces, both available formulations (absolute and ratio), and the inversion itself, on 100 subjects across 3 devices and 3 lighting conditions, with pre-declared thresholds in every case.

**Stated plainly and without softening: haemoglobin cannot be recovered from ambient-light RGB photographs of the eye at the accuracy any deployment would need, and the reasons are now quantified rather than speculated.** The eye does carry its own reference surfaces, and the illuminant does cancel in a ratio exactly as the algebra promises - but the reference surfaces are not stable enough across captures of the same person for either route to work, and the haemoglobin signal is far below the residual error either route leaves.

No third reformulation is proposed.

### What survives, and it is not nothing

* **The PPG arm is unaffected and is now co-primary.** Four narrow wavelengths, a controlled source, and no ambient illuminant to estimate - the entire error term that defeated Phases 2, 2.5, 3 and 3.5 does not exist there. It is also the project's best-labelled data: 252 subjects with venous HemoCue reference.
* **N1a stands**: 0.666 deg angular error across six sensors given a reference of known reflectance.
* **The segmentation pipeline stands**: sclera IoU 0.875, 0% failure on Eyes-Defy.
* **A quantified account of why four plausible approaches fail**, each with a mechanism and a pre-declared threshold. Most of the literature this project set out to improve on reports single-site accuracy without cross-device validation; this is a more honest contribution than that, even though it is negative.
