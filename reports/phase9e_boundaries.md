# Phase 9E - the project's boundaries, stated precisely

Generated by `scripts/phase9e_report.py` from `data/interim/phase9e/`. **Everything this phase was allowed and not allowed to do was declared in CLAUDE.md and committed (`f4a3fc0`) before any script here ran** - the pre-declaration is verbatim in `docs/archive/predeclarations.md` section 10.

This phase builds no model, collects no data and **changes no verdict**. It names three things the record left implicit: the capture regime a real product uses and this project never measured; the sample a confirmatory study would need; and the statements that claimed more precision, or more absoluteness, than the evidence behind them.

> ## The three boundaries, in one screen
>
> **A. The guided-capture gap.** A deployed screening app captures with one phone in one session behind a framing overlay and a quality gate. That regime sits between two measured conditions - 1.981 and 1.062 dE2000 - **and is the one regime this project did not measure.** It is also exactly the interval in which the gate crosses out of NOT RECOVERABLE, and exactly where Phase 9C found the verdict fragile. It cannot be closed here. It is recorded as a named boundary and the **primary future-work item**.
>
> **B. What a confirmatory study would need.** The cross-site failure is a **composition** problem, not a sample-size one. `italy_to_india` estimates specificity on **27 non-anaemic subjects** and needs **266 on the 1/sqrt(n) reference, or 778 on the measured slope** - test sites of roughly 936 and 2736 people at that direction's prevalence, against 95 here. **Both figures appear wherever the requirement does**; the rule that picks between them was added mid-run and is set out in Part B.
>
> **C. What the record claimed too precisely.** Cross-site rates were quoted bare; underpowered nulls were written as absences; the studio gate figure was quoted without its interval. All three are corrected. **The two regression arms are ADEQUATELY POWERED and keep their full strength** - calibration runs in both directions.

## Part A - the guided-capture gap

### What was measured, and what a product actually does

Phase 7 measured the within-subject residual colour error under three capture conditions. A deployed app operates in none of them:

| capture condition | residual dE2000 | measured? | what it corresponds to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MOBIUS, 3 phones x 3 lighting | 3.456 | **yes** | photographs taken however they happen to be taken |
| MOBIUS, one phone + one lighting cell, gaze varying | 1.981 | **yes** | the same device and light, but nothing controlling how the shot is framed |
| **guided capture** | *1.450 (interpolated)* | **NO** | *one phone, one session, a live overlay enforcing framing and distance, and a quality gate rejecting blurred or badly exposed frames before the shutter* |
| SBVPI studio rig | 1.062 | **yes** | a fixed rig, which no screening deployment has |

**Why this gap is the uncomfortable one.** It is not merely an untested condition; it is the condition where the answer is closest to changing. The gate crosses from NOT RECOVERABLE into MARGINAL inside this interval, and Phase 9C measured the studio verdict at its lower end as FRAGILE - 27% of the parameter prior VIABLE, 36% MARGINAL, 37% NOT RECOVERABLE. **The regime where real products operate is the one regime this project did not measure, and it is the one where the answer is closest to changing.**

### It is not closed here, and it cannot be

It requires capturing images under that protocol; CLAUDE.md section 3 forbids collecting data, permanently. This is stated as a permanent boundary of the claim, not as a task deferred to a later phase of this project. There is no later phase in which it becomes possible.

### The interpolation, and what it is not

**It is an interpolation between two measured points. It is not a measurement of guided capture, and nothing in this repository should ever read as though it were.** The headline point is the geometric mean of the bracketing pair, **1.450 dE2000**, fixed in the pre-declaration before the gate was run at it. Fresh perturbation banks were drawn (seed 20260921) because no original RNG order exists at a residual nobody measured, and **the two measured anchors were re-run on those same fresh banks**: they land within **0.018 g/dL** of their recorded values, so the interpolated points are not an artefact of a different random draw.

| residual dE2000 | status | gate MAE g/dL (nominal theta) | band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.062 | **MEASURED anchor** | 1.057 | MARGINAL |
| 1.200 | *interpolated* | 1.194 | MARGINAL |
| 1.450 | *interpolated (headline)* | 1.445 | MARGINAL |
| 1.700 | *interpolated* | 1.686 | MARGINAL |
| 1.981 | **MEASURED anchor** | 2.045 | NOT RECOVERABLE |

**The interval, not just the point.** The Phase 9C prior over the forward model's 14 fixed tissue parameters was propagated at the headline point using **Phase 9C's own 2,048 prior draws**, so the interval is directly comparable to the recorded ones. The upper bracket, which had no interval on record, was propagated too:

| residual | status | point (nominal) | median | 95% interval | VIABLE / MARGINAL / NOT RECOVERABLE | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.450 | *interpolated* | 1.445 | 2.06 | [0.83, 5.73] | 9% / 39% / 52% | FRAGILE at 2.0; FRAGILE at 1.0 |
| 1.981 | measured | 2.045 | 2.76 | [1.17, 6.48] | 1% / 29% / 71% | FRAGILE at 2.0; ROBUST at 1.0 |

**What the interpolation says, honestly.** At nominal parameters the whole bracketing interval is MARGINAL and tips into NOT RECOVERABLE at its uncontrolled end. Under the prior the headline point is median 2.06 g/dL - **above the 2.0 line** - with 52% of the prior NOT RECOVERABLE and only 9% VIABLE; it is FRAGILE at 2.0; FRAGILE at 1.0. And the ceiling is hard: **VIABLE requires a residual below 0.986 dE2000, which is below the best measured condition in the entire project - a studio rig. No point in the guided-capture interval reaches VIABLE, including its most favourable end.** Guided capture plausibly lands MARGINAL at nominal parameters; it does not plausibly land VIABLE.

### What evidence would settle it

Stated so that someone who *can* collect data knows exactly what to collect, and so that the result would move the verdict in **either** direction rather than only the favourable one.

|  | specification |
| --- | --- |
| **capture protocol** | One phone model. One session per subject. A live on-screen overlay enforcing framing and camera-to-eye distance, and an automatic quality gate rejecting blurred or badly exposed frames before the shutter - i.e. the protocol a deployed app would ship, not a laboratory approximation of it. Ambient illumination left uncontrolled, because deployment cannot control it. |
| **the measurement** | The same quantity Phase 7 measured, computed the same way: the **within-subject spread (mean pairwise CIEDE2000) of segmented scleral colour across repeat captures of the same person**, under the best of {no correction, grey-world full frame, grey-world 25% crop}. Repeat captures within one session are required - a single image per subject yields no within-subject residual, which is why Eyes-Defy could not supply this. |
| **sample** | At least 3 repeat captures for each of >= 30 subjects, so the per-subject spread is estimated on more than a pair; subjects spanning the skin-tone range, because melanin is the 1st-ranked sensitivity at the studio residual (Phase 9C). This is a measurement of capture variability, not of haemoglobin, so **no blood draw is needed** - which is what makes it far cheaper than the confirmatory study in Part B. |
| **result that WEAKENS the refutation** | A residual **below 0.986 dE2000**, which would put the gate in VIABLE at nominal parameters and make the refutation specific to uncontrolled and semi-controlled capture. Note this is below the studio rig's measured residual, so it would be a genuinely surprising result. |
| **result that STRENGTHENS it** | A residual **at or above 1.981 dE2000** - i.e. guided capture buying nothing over the same phone and lighting with gaze varying - which would put the gate in NOT RECOVERABLE and make the refutation general to photographs. |
| **result that leaves it where it is** | Anything in between, i.e. MARGINAL, which the interpolation says is the likeliest outcome. **This is the expected result and it would still not make the arm deployable**, because MARGINAL does not beat site + sex + age and because the binding failure is cross-site transfer (Part B), which a single-site capture study does not address. |

**And the honest caveat on all of the above.** Even a VIABLE calibration residual would not settle the arm. Phase 6.5 measured a **between-subject** conjunctival colour term of ~4.5 dE2000 at fixed haemoglobin, sex, age and site - a second noise source the gate model never contained and that no amount of capture control removes. Capture control addresses one of the two terms.

## Part B - what a confirmatory study would need

Phase 9D found three comparisons UNDERPOWERED and stopped there. An UNDERPOWERED verdict without a required n is a complaint; with one it is a specification. Phase 9D's MDEs were **reproduced before any new number was computed** (`scripts/phase9e_required_n.py` asserts it), exactly as Phase 9D reproduced Phase 9A's.

### The method, and the assumption it does not make

Required group size = observed group size x (observed MDE / 0.10) ** (1 / slope), targeting the **0.10 margin pre-declared in Phase 9A**. That rests on the SE scaling as n to the power -slope. **The slope was measured, not assumed:** this project's own data was subsampled at 0.4, 0.55, 0.7, 0.85, 1.0 of each carrier group, stratified on the anaemia label so prevalence stays fixed, the entire nested-operating-point and paired-bootstrap pipeline was re-run at each, and log SE was fitted against log n.

| comparison | carrier group | n range probed | measured slope | fit R2 | trusted? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cross-site italy -> india - specificity at matched sensitivity | non-anaemic | 11-27 | 0.340 | 0.25 | no |
| cross-site india -> italy - specificity at matched sensitivity | non-anaemic | 39-98 | 0.432 | 0.71 | **yes** |
| imaging screening, within site - specificity at matched sensitivity | non-anaemic | 50-125 | 0.538 | 0.91 | **yes** |
| imaging screening, within site - sensitivity at matched specificity | anaemic | 36-91 | 0.480 | 0.75 | **yes** |
| PPG screening - AUROC vs the best demographic baseline | anaemic | 7-18 | 0.411 | 0.23 | no |

**The scaling assumption holds where it can be checked, and fails where the data is thinnest - which is itself informative.** On the two comparisons with a reasonably sized carrier group the measured slopes are 0.538 and 0.480, bracketing the textbook 0.5. On the two smallest - `italy_to_india`'s 27 non-anaemic subjects and the PPG arm's 18 anaemic ones - the fit collapses (R2 0.25 and 0.23), because there is not enough range to fit a slope over. **That is the same composition problem showing up a second time: these groups are too small even to measure how their own precision scales.** Where the fit is not trustworthy the ordinary 1/sqrt(n) rate is used for the headline and the measured figure is reported beside it. The rule is applied by R2, not by which answer is preferred.

### Required sample size at the 0.10 margin

**Where the R2 floor chose between two figures, both appear in the cell.** The headline is first; the alternative basis follows in brackets. Nothing below can be read without seeing both.

| comparison | carrier group | n now | **needed, 80%** | needed, 90% | multiple | cohort @ observed prevalence | cohort @ 50% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cross-site **italy -> india** - specificity at matched sensitivity | non-anaemic | 27 | **266** *(measured slope: 778)* | 356 *(measured slope: 1193)* | 9.9x | 936 *(measured slope: 2736)* | 532 *(measured slope: 1555)* |
| cross-site **india -> italy** - specificity at matched sensitivity | non-anaemic | 98 | **311** | 435 | 3.2x | 384 | 621 |
| imaging screening, within site - specificity at matched sensitivity | non-anaemic | 125 | **166** | 217 | 1.3x | 286 | 331 |
| imaging screening, within site - sensitivity at matched specificity | anaemic | 91 | **120** | 162 | 1.3x | 284 | 239 |
| PPG screening - AUROC vs the best demographic baseline | anaemic | 18 | **131** *(measured slope: 202)* | 176 *(measured slope: 288)* | 7.3x | 1839 *(measured slope: 2831)* | 263 *(measured slope: 404)* |

> ### Both figures, side by side - where the R2 floor chose
>
> **On 2 of the 5 comparisons the required n depends on a rule that was added after the analysis began, and the two candidate answers differ by up to threefold.** A reader must be able to see both without going looking, so they are stated together here and in every table above.
>
> **The rule.** Required n scales as (observed MDE / 0.10) raised to 1/slope. The slope was measured by subsampling rather than assumed. Where the subsample fit is itself weak - **R2 below 0.50** - the measured slope is not a reliable input, so the ordinary **1/sqrt(n) reference (slope 0.5)** becomes the headline and the measured-slope figure is reported beside it.
>
> **When it was fixed, stated plainly.** The pre-declaration (`docs/archive/predeclarations.md` section 10) said only that a slope departing materially from 0.5 would be reported as approximate with the measured value stated. It did not anticipate the fit *failing*. **The R2 floor was therefore added mid-run**, after the subsampling had been done, and logged as a deviation in the DECISION LOG of 2026-09-21 rather than applied silently.
>
> **And it happened to select the more optimistic figure in both cases.** That is stated because it is true and because it is the obvious objection. The rule keys on R2 alone and never on the size of the answer - a test asserts that the headline tracks R2 and that both bases stay on the record - but a rule chosen mid-run that lands on the smaller number twice deserves to be seen, not explained away.
>
> | comparison | carrier | R2 of the fit | measured slope | **headline (1/sqrt(n))** | alternative (measured slope) | ratio |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | cross-site italy -> india - specificity at matched sensitivity | non-anaemic | **0.25** | 0.340 | **266** at 80%, 356 at 90% | 778 at 80%, 1193 at 90% | 2.9x |
> | PPG screening - AUROC vs the best demographic baseline | anaemic | **0.23** | 0.411 | **131** at 80%, 176 at 90% | 202 at 80%, 288 at 90% | 1.5x |
>
> **Read the italy -> india requirement as 266 non-anaemic subjects on the 1/sqrt(n) reference and 778 on the measured slope** - cohorts of roughly 936 and 2736 at that direction's prevalence. **A study designer should size against the larger of the two unless they can measure the scaling on their own pilot**, which is the conservative reading and the one this report recommends, even though the smaller figure is the headline by the stated rule.


### The composition point, which matters more than any total here

**Phase 9D established that `italy_to_india`'s 14% power comes from composition, not raw n.** Its test site had 95 subjects, which is not tiny - but 68 of them were anaemic, so **specificity rested on 27 people**. A study designer who reads only the total will build the wrong study.

| direction | test n | anaemic | **non-anaemic now** | **minimum non-anaemic required, 80%** | at 90% | implied test-site size at that prevalence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| italy -> india | 95 | 68 | 27 | **266** *(measured slope: 778)* | 356 *(measured slope: 1193)* | 936 *(measured slope: 2736)* |
| india -> italy | 121 | 23 | 98 | **311** | 435 | 384 |

**The minimum non-anaemic count per direction is the number to design against**: for italy -> india, **266** on the 1/sqrt(n) reference or **778** on the measured slope the R2 floor set aside (see the box above; size against the larger unless you can measure the scaling on your own pilot); for india -> italy, **311**, whose fit was trustworthy so the question does not arise. On the reference basis the two land close together, which is the expected result if the per-subject variance is similar in both directions and the difference really is composition - a consistency check the analysis passes. Recruiting to a balanced prevalence rather than accepting each site's natural one cuts italy -> india's cohort from 936 to about 532.

### Caveats, carried rather than buried

* The per-subject model scores are held fixed while subjects are subsampled; a real smaller study would also retrain, and its model would be worse, so these are LOWER bounds on n.
* The SE of a proportion depends mildly on the true effect size, which is not corrected for (Phase 9D caveat, carried).
* A larger study drawn from more sites would have a different per-subject variance; this extrapolates THIS cohort.
* The Phase 9D caveat travels with these figures unchanged: the SE is estimated from this sample and carries its own sampling uncertainty.

**Read these as lower bounds.** The subsampling holds the per-subject model scores fixed; a real smaller study would also have retrained on less data and produced a worse model. A real larger study drawn from more sites would face a different, probably larger, per-subject variance than this cohort's.

## Part C - bounding the conclusions to the evidence

Three specific overstatements, corrected wherever they appeared. **Generating scripts were edited and the reports regenerated; no report was hand-patched.**

### 1. Cross-site numbers quoted as precise estimates

Phase 9D established that the magnitude of the cross-site penalty is poorly pinned down. The record nonetheless quoted rates bare. **Observed counts carry no estimation uncertainty and now lead; every rate carries its interval.** Phase 9A had stored a CI for the cross-site *sensitivity* only, so `scripts/phase9e_intervals.py` computed the rest, reproducing Phase 9A's confusion matrices and sensitivity intervals exactly before adding anything.

| direction | the count (preferred) | sensitivity (95% CI) | specificity (95% CI) | estimated on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| india -> italy | **flagged 21 of 23 anaemic subjects and 57 of 98 non-anaemic subjects** | 0.913 [0.783, 1.000] | 0.418 [0.323, 0.516] | 23 anaemic / 98 non-anaemic |
| italy -> india | **flagged 27 of 68 anaemic subjects and 1 of 27 non-anaemic subjects** | 0.397 [0.278, 0.514] | 0.963 [0.885, 1.000] | 68 anaemic / 27 non-anaemic |

**The verdict does not change and was never in question.** Sensitivity 0.397 [0.278, 0.514] - 27 of 68 anaemic subjects flagged, 41 missed - was *observed*; a power analysis constrains what can be concluded from *null* comparisons, not from an observed collapse. What changes is only the precision claimed for the magnitude. `italy_to_india` specificity 0.963 looks decisive until one notices it rests on 27 subjects and its interval reaches 1.000.

### 2. Underpowered nulls stated as absence

A null from an underpowered comparison is a **bound**, not an absence, and is now written as one.

| comparison | was written as | now written as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| imaging screening within site (CNN) | "no gain was found" | "no specificity gain of 0.10 or larger was detected; this design could have detected 0.116" (67% power at the margin) |
| imaging screening within site, sensitivity direction | same | "...could have detected 0.114" |
| PPG screening AUROC | "AUROC diff -0.001, CI spans zero" - used as confirmation | uninformative on its own: MDE 0.27 on 18 anaemic subjects. **The PPG verdict rests on its MAE and NNS figures, and nothing leans on its AUROC.** |

**What was NOT weakened, deliberately.** The two regression arms are ADEQUATELY POWERED - MDE 0.181 g/dL for imaging and 0.137 for PPG, against a narrowest WHO band of 1.0 g/dL. Those negative results are informative evidence of absence, and the effects too small for this design to detect are also far too small to matter clinically. **Hedging them would be the same error in the opposite direction, and it was checked for.** The PPG *specificity* comparison (MDE 0.073) is likewise adequately powered.

### 3. The studio result quoted without its interval

Phase 7's studio gate figure of **1.040 g/dL** sits 0.04 above the VIABLE line - a residual 1.08x what VIABLE needs, which reads as *within 8% of viable*. **That reading survives only at the nominal parameter choice.** Over the Phase 9C prior the same residual gives median 1.52, 95% [0.60, 4.90] - 27.1% VIABLE, 35.9% MARGINAL, 37.0% NOT RECOVERABLE. Its band is a property of the parameter choice as much as of the capture condition. `reports/phase7.md` now carries the interval in the gate table itself, on every measured and interpolated row, and the *within 8% of viable* sentence carries the interval in the same breath.

### What the pass found that was not on the list

* **A correction that had not propagated.** `reports/phase7.md` still carried the clause "and moves no clinical threshold", which Phase 9A retired on 2026-09-17 (`docs/archive/corrections.md` section 8). The correction had been recorded in the archive and in CLAUDE.md but never applied to the Phase 7 generator. It is now removed, with a note in its place saying what stood there and why it went.
* **The audit package carried none of the three overstatements.** Neither `hemosight.audit.content` nor `/api/case-study` quotes a cross-site rate, an underpowered null or the studio figure, so there was nothing to correct. What they lacked was any statement of the boundaries at all - a worked example that shows only what a project found, and never what it could not have found, teaches half the lesson - so `/api/case-study` now serves a `boundaries` block read from the Phase 9D and 9E artefacts.
* **The landing page's noise-versus-signal ladder** now shows the guided-capture regime as a dashed outline labelled *interpolated*, beside the three measured bars. Leaving it out is what let the gap go unnoticed.

## What a confirmatory study would need - Parts A and B together

**These are two different studies, and conflating them is how the project's own record came to leave both implicit.** One is cheap and answers a physics question. The other is expensive and answers the deployment question. **The second is the one that decides the arm**, and doing only the first would repeat this project's mistake of measuring a single site well.

|  | **Study 1 - the capture-residual study (Part A)** | **Study 2 - the cross-site screening study (Part B)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **question it answers** | What residual does guided capture actually leave? I.e. is the imaging refutation about photographs or about uncontrolled photographs? | Does a within-site screening advantage survive transfer to another site? I.e. is the arm deployable? |
| **protocol** | One phone, one session, live framing/distance overlay, automatic quality gate, ambient light uncontrolled. Repeat captures per subject. | The same guided protocol, applied at **two or more sites with different populations**, with the operating point chosen on one site and applied to the other - the construction Phase 9A used. |
| **measurement** | Within-subject mean pairwise CIEDE2000 of segmented scleral colour, best of {none, grey-world full, grey-world 25% crop}. Then the Phase 3 gate re-run at that measured residual. | Specificity at matched sensitivity against a **site + sex + age** baseline on identical folds, with a subject-level paired bootstrap CI, against the 0.10 margin. Venous haemoglobin reference required. |
| **sample** | >= 3 repeat captures for each of >= 30 subjects, spanning the skin-tone range. **No blood draw needed.** | **Per direction: at least 266 non-anaemic subjects in the test site on the 1/sqrt(n) reference, or 778 on the measured slope** (and >= 120 anaemic for the sensitivity direction). At a natural high-prevalence site that is a ~936- to ~2736-person cohort; recruited to a balanced prevalence, about 532 to 1555. **Size against the larger figure unless you can measure the scaling on your own pilot.** **Both sites must meet the non-anaemic floor, because transfer is tested in both directions.** |
| **cost driver** | Number of repeat captures, not subjects. | **The non-anaemic count, not the total.** This is the specific thing this project got wrong: 95 test subjects of which 68 were anaemic. |
| **what would change the verdict** | A residual below 0.986 dE2000 would move the gate to VIABLE and narrow the refutation to uncontrolled capture. Anything above leaves it where it is. | A specificity gain >= 0.10 with a CI lower bound above zero, **holding in both directions**, at sensitivity >= 0.90 and specificity >= 0.50. Anything less leaves the arm not deployable. |
| **what it would still not settle** | The ~4.5 dE2000 between-subject conjunctival colour term at fixed Hb, sex, age and site (Phase 6.5), which capture control does not touch. | Severe anaemia, which no trusted dataset in this project contains (minimum 7.0 g/dL), and pregnancy status, which neither dataset records. |

**And the finding that should govern how either study is read.** This project's best-powered comparisons are its regression ones, and they are unambiguous: no method in six attempts across two modalities beat **sex alone, MAE 0.831 g/dL**. A confirmatory study that does not carry a demographic baseline on identical folds will not be able to tell a haemoglobin estimator from a sex classifier - which is the failure mode this project measured directly, and which **0 of the 7 papers read in Phase 9B report a baseline against**.

**No verdict, anywhere.** The imaging arm remains CLOSED, FINAL; the PPG arm remains NOT VIABLE; the Phase 3 gate remains NOT RECOVERABLE and ROBUST to parameter uncertainty; the studio condition remains MARGINAL and FRAGILE; the one positive claim remains real and clinically useless. Phase 9E was pre-declared as forbidden to change a verdict, and it did not.
